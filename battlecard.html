Objection handling

Frozen is not safe

Banks are secure. Concede that immediately. The question a treasurer should ask is what that security does to their working capital. Legacy compliance runs after the payment leaves, so a flag freezes funds mid-flight with no visibility to the sender. Sokin's screening clears the payment before the money moves. The pitch is certainty, not security.

The freeze

"Our bank held a supplier payment for nine days and could not tell us why."

Why it happens

Legacy screening runs after initiation. The payment leaves, a rule fires somewhere along the route, and the funds stop in a place the sender cannot see. The bank often cannot explain the hold, because the hold may not be theirs.

What the seller says

"With pre-flight screening, the transaction clears before it leaves. If something needs checking, it gets checked while your money is still yours."

The multiplier

"Why does the same corridor behave differently every month?"

Why it happens

A legacy cross-border payment can route through two or three correspondent banks. Each runs its own screening, on its own rules, on its own calendar. Every additional intermediary is another independent point at which the payment can be held, at a bank the sender has no relationship with and no number to call.

What the seller says

"There is no chain of intermediaries here. One counterparty, one screening pass, one relationship to call if you ever need to."

The onboarding drag

"Opening the corridor account took our bank eleven weeks."

Why it happens

Bank KYC is manual and document-led: certified copies, wet signatures, sequential review queues. The checks themselves are legitimate and necessary. The delivery mechanism is what is slow.

What the seller says

"The same checks run through an API-led process, so onboarding is measured in days rather than weeks. Nothing is skipped. It is the queue that disappears, not the diligence."

Why this exists

This page argues a position: never fight an incumbent on its strongest attribute. A challenger who claims to be more secure than a global bank loses the room. A challenger who concedes security and attacks what that security costs the client's working capital changes what the deal is about. That reframe is the whole page.

Every argument here is structural, not statistical. No failure rates, no percentages. If the mechanism is right, the prospect supplies their own evidence from their own payment history, which is more persuasive than any number a vendor could quote.
