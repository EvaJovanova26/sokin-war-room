Qualification pipeline · account to sequence

How an account becomes ICP

The scorer on tab 3 shows the decision. This page shows the machinery behind it: every step an account passes through, every gate that can stop it, and where each piece of data lives in the CRM so the model stays alive after launch week.

1
Step · Enter the book

The account exists in the CRM

An account record with the minimum firmographic fields filled: industry, size, headquarters, corridors touched. No scoring happens yet. An account that is not in the book cannot be qualified, which sounds obvious until you audit how many deals started outside the CRM and never got a record.

!
Gate · Sanctions screen

Prohibited jurisdiction check, before anything else

If a sanctioned or prohibited jurisdiction sits anywhere in the payment flow, the account exits the pipeline here, permanently, regardless of how attractive it looks on every other measure. This gate runs first because no downstream signal is allowed to argue with it.

Fails Flagged, closed, not revisited Passes Continues to fit
2
Gate · Structural fit

Could this ever be a good customer?

Three fields, set from what the company is rather than what it does this week: corridor exposure, annual cross-border volume band, company size. Fit is slow-moving, so these fields are set once and refreshed on a schedule rather than watched daily.

High fit Continues to triggers Low fit Automated nurture only, never staffed
3
Gate · Live triggers

Is something happening right now?

Observable events, logged as fields on the account: a new finance or treasury leader, an ERP migration underway, hiring for international expansion, multiple providers stitched together. Fit says the account could buy. Triggers say it could buy now. The gate is a threshold, not a judgement call: enough live triggers alongside high fit, and the account qualifies.

Clears Qualified, a seller owns it Short Nurture, and watch for the next event
✓
State · ICP, in sequence

Pushed to the corridor sequence

The account drops into the corridor sequence with the business case calculator pre-loaded on its own numbers, and a named seller owns it from this moment. Qualification is not a report line; it is a handover with a deadline attached.

↻
Loop · The model is audited

Monthly: do the signals still predict revenue?

Once a month, across the whole book, two questions: are the signals that gate the pipeline actually correlated with wins, and is the data behind them being maintained? A qualification model nobody audits decays into folklore within two quarters. The model performance view is that audit.

How it lives in the CRM

Every input above is a field on the account record with a named owner and an update rhythm. This is what separates a model from a slide: the slide describes it once, the CRM keeps it true.

Fit fields

Set at creation, refreshed quarterly

Corridor exposure, volume band, company size. Populated when the record is created, from enrichment and first-call notes, then reviewed on a quarterly cadence because structure changes slowly.

Owner · RevOps, with enrichment tooling Rhythm · Quarterly review
Trigger fields

Event-driven, logged as they happen

Leadership changes, ERP migrations, expansion hiring, provider sprawl. Reps log what they hear on calls; support tickets and public signals feed the rest. A trigger field untouched for a quarter is treated as stale, not as a quiet no.

Owner · Reps and support, shared Rhythm · As events occur, staleness flagged
The score

Computed, never typed

Qualification is a formula over the fields, not an opinion typed into a picklist. Nobody hand-edits a score; if the verdict looks wrong, the fix is a field or the model, and both leave an audit trail.

Owner · The model, versioned Rhythm · Recomputed on any field change
The sequence

Qualification triggers the handover

Clearing the gate fires the corridor sequence automatically and assigns an owner. The account cannot sit qualified and unowned, because a qualified account with no next step is pipeline evaporating quietly.

Owner · The assigned seller Rhythm · Immediate on qualification
Process description, not reporting. Field names and cadences get finalised with RevOps in the first 30 days.
