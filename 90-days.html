The first 90 days

Each phase answers a specific requirement in the job description with a dated deliverable. The commitments below are limited to what the role actually controls: assets shipped, calls attended, pipeline sourced, partners in onboarding, evidence produced.

Days 1 to 30

Audit, not onboarding

JD: buyer insight · sales enablement

Sit in on live sales calls. Interview the top sellers about where deals slow down and why. Mine the lost-deal record for the objections that actually killed revenue, rather than the ones people remember killing it.

Shipped by day 30: the cost of waiting calculator and the three objection plays, in sellers' hands, with compliance sign-off. External-facing collateral in a regulated business ships only after the compliance team has reviewed it, so that review is booked in week one, not discovered in week three.

Days 31 to 60

Run the wedge

JD: campaign design and execution

Launch phase 1 of the Weekend Test in the chosen corridor: the audit motion in market, sequences live, outreach keyed to month-end.

Shipped by day 60: design partners identified from the existing customer book, agreed in principle, and moving into onboarding. Onboarding a regulated customer takes weeks, so the honest day-60 commitment is partners in motion, not volume settled.

Days 61 to 90

Turn evidence into routes

JD: product feedback · partner marketing

Feed what the pilot conversations surface back to product as structured roadmap input. Open the partner-led route with ERP and accounting platforms, starting from whatever evidence exists by then rather than waiting for it to be complete.

Shipped by day 90: the corridor motion documented end to end, so the second corridor is a repeat rather than a reinvention. Platform partnerships take quarters; the day-90 deliverable is the route opened and the playbook written, not the deal signed.

And a number on the wall that I own.

An outcome target set with Finance inside the first 30 days, then made public internally. The commitment is to owning the process of setting the number, with the people who hold the data, rather than inventing a number before day one.

Why this exists

Deliverables, not adjectives

Every phase ends in something that exists: an asset in sellers' hands, a campaign in market, a documented motion. A 90-day plan that cannot be checked against reality at day 91 is not a plan.

Scoped to the role

Nothing here promises what sales cycles, customer onboarding or platform partnerships would have to deliver on someone else's timeline. Panels stop believing a plan at the first promise its author cannot keep alone.

Compliance as collaborator

Naming who signs off external collateral, and when, is cheap to write and expensive to omit. It signals that the constraint is understood before it is encountered.
